This project quantifies and maps soil organic carbon (SOC) to support soil health assessment and climate mitigation through improved carbon accounting and targeted management. SOC is highly variable due to interacting controls such as climate, topography, vegetation, parent material, and land use, so sparse sampling and coarse maps can misrepresent true carbon patterns. We integrate SOC measurements (and, where available, depth information and supporting attributes needed for stock estimation) with environmental covariates including terrain derivatives, climate variables, land cover proxies, geology, and remote-sensing predictors such as SWIR bands, time series metrics, and bare-soil composites. Machine learning and ensemble approaches are used to capture nonlinear responses and interactions, with spatially informed validation to ensure performance estimates reflect real mapping and transfer scenarios. Where feasible, SOC concentration predictions are converted into carbon stock surfaces by accounting for depth intervals and bulk density assumptions or measurements, enabling reporting at field, watershed, or regional scales. Deliverables include SOC/stock maps and reliability layers that highlight robust areas versus locations needing more data. The project supports climate-smart actions by identifying zones with high sequestration potential, areas vulnerable to carbon loss, and management priorities for sustainable land stewardship.
